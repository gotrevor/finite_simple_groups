## §1-bridge. Restriction of scalars via the trace form (answers "apply bilinear machinery to a Hermitian form")

Let `E = F_{q²}`, `σ` the q-Frobenius involution, `F = F_q` its fixed field (`[E:F]=2`,
separable). For the standard Hermitian form `H(x,y) = Σ σ(xᵢ) yᵢ` on `Eⁿ`, define the
**F-bilinear trace form** on `V` viewed as a `2n`-dim `F`-space (`restrictScalars`):

```
B(x,y) := Tr_{E/F}( H(x,y) )
```

- `B` is **symmetric F-bilinear** (H Hermitian + `Tr ∘ σ = Tr`).
- `B` **nondegenerate over F ⟺ H nondegenerate over E** (the trace pairing `Tr_{E/F}` is
  nondegenerate because `E/F` is separable).
- For an `E`-subspace `W`: the **E-orthogonal complement of `W` w.r.t. `H` equals the
  F-orthogonal complement w.r.t. `B`** — because `H(x,y)=0 ⟺ Tr(λ·H(x,y))=0 ∀λ∈E` (trace
  pairing nondegeneracy), and the `F`-perp of an `E`-subspace is automatically an `E`-subspace.
- **Dimensions:** `dim_F V = 2·dim_E V`; mathlib's `finrank_orthogonal` over `F` gives
  `dim_F W⊥ = 2·dim_E V − 2·dim_E W`, hence `dim_E W⊥ = dim_E V − dim_E W = n − dim_E W`.
  For a hyperbolic plane `W` (`dim_E 2`): **`dim_E W⊥ = n − 2`.** ✓
- **`V = W ⊕ W⊥`** when `H|W` is nondegenerate (a hyperbolic plane is): `Disjoint` from the
  nondegenerate restriction, `Codisjoint` from the `finrank` count.

mathlib decls for the bridge: `Algebra.trace` / `Algebra.traceForm`
(`Mathlib/RingTheory/Trace/`), `Algebra.traceForm_nondegenerate` (separable),
`Submodule.restrictScalars`, `Module.finrank_mul_finrank` (the `[E:F]·dim_E = dim_F` tower).

⚠️ **Caveat — you work in coordinates, not abstract `BilinForm`.** Your model is
`star u ⬝ᵥ v` over `Fin n` with the identity Gram matrix, and you've **already** hand-built the
perp infra (`uForm_nondegenerate`, `offSU`/`FixSU`). So the abstract bridge is most valuable if
you ever abstract the form; in coordinates the same `n−2` is the concrete fact "`W⊥` is the
kernel of the `2×n` matrix `[e f]ᴴ` of rank 2, so nullity `= n−2`". Either path gives `n−2`;
**neither needs Witt.**

## §3 (LIVE). Generation of SU by unitary transvections — the textbook theorem + the induction

### The theorem
**Theorem (Dieudonné).** Let `V` be a nondegenerate Hermitian space over `F_{q²}` of dimension
`n ≥ 2` containing a nonzero isotropic vector. Then the **unitary transvections generate
`SU(V)`**, with the **single exception `(n,q) = (3,2)`** (i.e. `SU₃(2)` over `F₄`).

- The exception being *exactly* `(n,q)=(3,2)` is confirmed by web sources. **Your `p ≥ 5`
  (so `q = p ≥ 5`) and `n ≥ 3` are safely outside it.** (`SU₃(2)` is solvable; `PSU₃(2)` is
  not simple — this is the same small-case carve-out as `PSU₃(2)` in the simplicity statement.)
- **Isotropic vector always exists for `n ≥ 2`:** every nondegenerate Hermitian form over
  `F_{q²}` is unitarily congruent to the standard one (Wikipedia, *Unitary group*), and that
  form is isotropic for `n ≥ 2`. (Witt index `= ⌊n/2⌋` — confidence ~85% on the exact index;
  the *existence* of an isotropic vector for `n ≥ 2` is certain, see the point count in §2b.)

### References (math source for the Lean proof / docstring citations)
- **Hahn–O'Meara, *The Classical Groups and K-Theory*** (Grundlehren 291, Springer 1989),
  **Thm 9.2.6**: `SU` is generated by **Eichler (Siegel) transformations** when the hyperbolic
  rank `≥ 1` and the **trace map `s ↦ s + s*` is surjective**. Over `F_{q²}` the trace
  `E → F` is surjective ✓ — and this is exactly your trace-zero centre condition
  `a + star a = 0` having a full image. This is the cleanest "ring-general, transvection-level"
  statement to cite.
- **Dieudonné, *Sur les générateurs des groupes classiques*,** Summa Bras. Math. 3 (1955)
  149–178; **Dieudonné, *La géométrie des groupes classiques*** (1955) — the original.
- **Grove, *Classical Groups and Geometric Algebra*** (AMS GSM 39, 2002), ch. 6 — modern,
  field-case, the move-by-move induction.
- **Taylor, *The Geometry of the Classical Groups*** (1992), ch. 11 — unitary specifically.

### Proof structure — the Eichler dimension induction (= your `UExactPairTrans`, port-ready)
This is the standard **non-Witt** route. It is precisely the reduction you already set up.

1. **(transitivity — you've done this)** The transvection group `T` acts transitively on
   hyperbolic pairs `(e,f)`, `⟨e,f⟩ = 1`, via **Eichler transformations** (each a product of
   ≤ 2 transvections). [your `exists_su_maps_nonorth`, the Weyl/swap 3-transvection product,
   `uEichler`.]
2. **(reduce to a pair stabilizer)** Given `g ∈ SU`, `(g·e, g·f)` is another hyperbolic pair;
   by (1) pick `t ∈ T` with `t·g` fixing `e` and `f`. So **WLOG `g` fixes `e,f` pointwise.**
3. **(restrict to the complement)** `g` fixing the hyperbolic plane `H = ⟨e,f⟩` pointwise ⟹ `g`
   preserves `W = H⊥` (dim `n−2`, nondegenerate Hermitian — §1-bridge) and
   `g|W ∈ SU(W)` (det balance: `det g = 1`, `g = 1` on `H` ⟹ `det(g|W) = 1`).
4. **(induct on n)** `g|W` is a product of transvections of `W`; each extends to a transvection
   of `V` with the **same centre** (a vector in `W = H⊥`), which **fixes `e,f` automatically**.
   Hence `g ∈ T`. Base case `dim ≤ 2`.

**Why `n ≥ 3` is essential** (and why `(3,2)` is the lone exception): in step 2, to fix the
hyperbolic pair *and* keep `det = 1` you need a **third dimension to absorb a norm-1 scalar** —
this is exactly your **`exists_scale` "third-dimension determinant balance"**. Over `F₄`
(`q=2`, `n=3`) there isn't enough room (`exists_fixedField_norm_ne_one` fails — every fixed-field
norm is 1), which is the structural reason `SU₃(2)` is the exception. `p ≥ 5` is precisely what
makes `exists_fixedField_norm_ne_one` go through.

**Same structure kills the symplectic `sp_stab_hyperbolic_le`:** the stabilizer of a hyperbolic
pair in `Sp` restricts to `Sp` on the complement; Deloro §6.4.4 (`β|W` nondeg ⟺ `V = W ⊕ W⊥`)
and §6.1.8 (`dim W⊥ = codim W`) are the bilinear facts (mathlib has both for `BilinForm`).
Symplectic transvections generate `Sp(2n,F)` for all `n,q` (no exceptions).

## §2b. Hermitian variety point counts (now moot for `hPP`, kept for completeness)

- Nondegenerate Hermitian variety in `PG(n−1, q²)` (`n` variables) has
  **`(qⁿ − (−1)ⁿ)(q^{n−1} − (−1)^{n−1}) / (q² − 1)`** projective points
  (Bose–Chakravarti; HandWiki/Wikipedia *Hermitian variety*).
- **Nonzero isotropic vectors in `Eⁿ` = `(q²−1)·`(that count)**; total incl. 0 =
  `1 + (q²−1)·(count)`. For `n ≥ 2` this is `> 0` ⟹ an isotropic vector exists (used in §3).
- **Chevalley–Warning does NOT rescue small `n`:** the norm form `Σ N(xᵢ)` has degree `q+1` in
  `2n` `F_q`-variables; CW needs `#vars > deg`, i.e. `2n > q+1`, which fails for small `n`/large
  `q`. So CW is the wrong tool; the explicit count above is what gives isotropy unconditionally.
  Reference for the counts: Hirschfeld, *Projective Geometries over Finite Fields*, Hermitian
  chapter.
